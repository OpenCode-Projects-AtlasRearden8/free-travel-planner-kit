How to use this sheet

Medications & Contacts Card · fold it in half, live in your wallet while traveling.

What goes on the card

Prescription names and doses, known allergies, and a short list of phone numbers. Keep it plain — a stranger filling in for you in a waiting room should be able to act on it.

Two copies, always

One travels in your wallet's outer pocket so it's reachable without digging through a carry-on in a fog. One stays with a trusted person at home. Shred both copies after the trip — it's personal information.

Not medical advice

This is an organizer, not a health authority. Pack enough cured medication per trip days plus buffer, and check your destination's rules for anything controlled. Ask your pharmacist or prescriber about refills, storage heat, or time zones — they actually enjoy being asked before, not after.

Original bottles, carry-on

Original labeled bottles beat a smooth-pill-organizer at any checkpoint. Keep them at the carry-on, not the checked bag — delays happen, and so does lost luggage.
